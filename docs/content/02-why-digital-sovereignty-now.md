## Defining Digital Sovereignty

**Digital sovereignty** is defined as:

> The ability of a state to **control and manage** their own **digital infrastructure, data, and technologies** without dependence on or **undue influence** from **foreign entities or external actors**.
>
> It encompasses the right to make decisions about **digital assets**, **data privacy**, **cybersecurity**, and the **use of technology**, ensuring that these decisions **align with local laws, interests, and values**.

This definition highlights several core dimensions:

- **Control over digital infrastructure** -- owning or governing the physical and virtual systems that underpin digital services
- **Data governance** -- determining how data is collected, stored, processed, and shared
- **Technology independence** -- reducing reliance on foreign technology providers
- **Alignment with values** -- ensuring technology deployment reflects local legal frameworks and societal norms

## Effects of Politics on Technology

Political decisions can have **significant and wide-ranging effects** on technology. Government policies and actions shape the **development**, **use**, and **impact** of technology through:

- **Funding and investment** -- government budgets directed toward R&D, startups, and infrastructure
- **Regulation and legislation** -- laws that govern data protection, AI usage, content moderation, and market competition
- **Education and workforce policies** -- shaping the talent pipeline for the tech sector
- **International relations and trade** -- tariffs, sanctions, and trade agreements that affect technology supply chains
- **Infrastructure decisions** -- choices about broadband deployment, data center locations, and 5G networks
- **Ethics and social responsibility** -- establishing boundaries for acceptable use of technology

This slide establishes the fundamental premise of the talk: politics and technology are deeply intertwined, and political decisions increasingly determine the trajectory of digital ecosystems.
