# European Digital Dependency

## Over 80% of Europe's Digital Technologies Are Imported

**Over 80% of Europe's digital technologies are imported.** These dependencies primarily involve the **United States** and **China**, as well as the major tech companies based in these countries.

This central finding, drawn from the **EuroStack 2025** report, frames the entire discussion of European digital dependency. The following sections break down this dependency across five critical dimensions: digital infrastructure, R&D investment, critical raw materials, microchips, and artificial intelligence.

---

## Digital Infrastructure

Amazon, Microsoft, and Google dominate **almost 70%** of the European cloud computing (**IaaS**) market, while Europe's largest cloud provider holds a mere **2% share**.

This staggering imbalance means that the vast majority of European organizations -- public and private alike -- rely on US-headquartered companies for the foundational infrastructure that powers their digital services, data storage, and computing needs.

> Source: The Draghi report on EU competitiveness

---

## R&D and Patenting

EU firms represent only **7% of global R&D spending** in software and internet technologies, compared to **71% by US** firms and **15% by Chinese** companies.

This enormous R&D gap reflects a structural deficit in Europe's ability to develop and commercialize the next generation of digital technologies. When the United States invests more than ten times what Europe does in software and internet R&D, the resulting innovation advantage compounds over time -- shaping global standards, attracting talent, and locking in market dominance.

> Source: European Investment Bank: Resilience and Renewal in Europe, Investment Report 2022/2023

---

## Critical Raw Materials

China controls about **90%** of global **rare earth refining capacity**. Key resources in which this dominance is felt include:

- **Lithium**
- **Cobalt**
- **Nickel**
- **Gallium**
- **Graphite**
- **Tungsten**

These materials are essential for manufacturing semiconductors, batteries, electronic components, and other hardware that underpins the entire digital economy. Europe's near-total reliance on Chinese refining for these inputs creates a serious vulnerability in the supply chain.

---

## Microchips

Europe consumes about **20% of the world's microchips** but **manufactures only 9%**.

This gap between consumption and production leaves Europe heavily dependent on foreign semiconductor supply chains -- particularly those running through Taiwan (TSMC), South Korea (Samsung), and the United States. The **European Chips Act** was proposed precisely to address this vulnerability by boosting domestic chip manufacturing capacity.

> Source: EUR-Lex - 52022DC0045 - A Chips Act for Europe

---

## Artificial Intelligence

Since 2017, **70% of foundational AI models** have been developed in the **United States** and **15% in China**.

This means that Europe has produced only a small fraction of the large-scale AI models that are increasingly shaping industries, government services, healthcare, defense, and everyday applications. The dominance of US companies (such as OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta, and Anthropic) and Chinese firms (such as Baidu and Alibaba) in foundational AI development puts Europe in a position of technological dependence for one of the most transformative technologies of the coming decades.

> Source: Large AI Models for Germany -- Feasibility Study 2023 -- LEAM.AI
