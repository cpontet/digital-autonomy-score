# EU Regulatory Framework

The European Union has built a comprehensive regulatory framework to protect its **strategic autonomy** in the digital domain. Through a combination of data protection law, AI regulation, and platform governance, the EU asserts its ability to set and enforce its own digital standards independently of non-EU tech powers.

## EU Strategic Autonomy

The EU already protects the strategic autonomy of its member states and citizens. Key aspects include:

- **Control over essential technologies** (e.g., cloud, AI, semiconductors)
- **Data sovereignty** -- keeping data within EU jurisdiction
- Reduced dependence on non-EU tech giants
- Ability to set and enforce own digital standards and regulations

## GDPR: Already Protecting You

The **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** contains powerful provisions that already shield European data from foreign overreach.

### Chapter V -- International Transfers

**GDPR Chapter V** restricts transfers of personal data outside the EU unless adequate protection is guaranteed. This chapter is the cornerstone of the EU's defense against uncontrolled data flows to third countries.

### Article 48 -- Foreign Court Orders

**Article 48** establishes that direct requests from non-EU authorities (like US law enforcement) are not a valid legal basis for data transfer unless based on an international agreement (like an **MLAT** -- Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty).

Key protections under Article 48 and related provisions:

- EU companies and subsidiaries are obligated to avoid illegal data transfers. They must challenge overly broad requests and seek legal remedies.
- Data can only be shared if strictly necessary, with narrow proportionality, and any transfer should be limited as much as possible.
- National data protection authorities can investigate, block, or sanction unlawful transfers to foreign governments.

## EU AI Act

The **European Union Artificial Intelligence Act** is the world's first comprehensive law specifically regulating artificial intelligence.

Its aim is to ensure that AI systems are safe, transparent, and respect fundamental rights.

The Act was formally adopted by the European Parliament in March 2024 and by the Council in May 2024, and will start to apply in stages, with the strictest requirements rolling out over the next few years.

## Digital Services Act (DSA)

The **Digital Services Act (DSA)** is a major piece of European Union legislation, adopted in 2022, that aims to create a safer and more transparent online environment. It is part of the EU's broader **Digital Services Package** alongside the **Digital Markets Act (DMA)**.

The DSA introduces new obligations for online platforms, especially large ones, with the goal of:

- **Protecting users' rights online**
- **Combating illegal content**
- **Improving transparency of algorithms and advertising**
- **Increasing accountability for big online platforms**
