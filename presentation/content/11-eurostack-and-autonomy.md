## EuroStack -- Core Principles

The EuroStack initiative is built on seven core principles:

- **Sovereignty and Security** -- Guaranteeing that Europe's critical digital infrastructure is under European jurisdiction, protected by robust security-by-design and privacy-by-design.

- **De-Proprietarization and Interoperability** -- Promoting integration across an open source, federated tech stack, while reducing reliance on Big Tech proprietary solutions.

- **Sustainability** -- Building energy-efficient and resource-resilient systems to meet Europe's Environmental and Climate objectives.

- **Data as a Common Good** -- Treating data as a shared resource to unlock innovation while safeguarding societal interests and fundamental rights.

- **Decentralized Sovereign Infrastructure** -- Combining edge computing and centralized systems for improved efficiency and data sovereignty.

- **Inclusive Governance** -- Ensuring harmonized regulations, accountability, and a balance between short-term resilience and long-term autonomy.

- **Strong Democracy** -- Digital technologies that do no harm, but fundamentally strengthen democratic societies.

## What About the Risk of Isolation?

We live in a globalized world already and we do not want to cut ourselves from it.

### Autonomy vs. Independence vs. Isolation

| Concept | Definition | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | Make your own decisions within certain constraints or broader systems | There is self-direction, but within ecosystem boundaries set by dominant platforms or regulators. |
| **Independence** | Operate entirely without relying on external platforms, providers, or authorities | Full control, no required interaction or compliance with any external digital authorities. |
| **Isolation** | Cut off from other digital actors or networks. Not just freedom, but lack of interaction or integration | No participation in the wider digital economy or ecosystem, which often limits growth and access to innovation. |

### The Guiding Framework

> **Ensure autonomy. Strive for independence. Prevent isolation.**

## The End-User Dilemma

The end-user dilemma illustrates the real-world tension between convenience, principles, and sovereignty in choosing digital platforms. The presentation compares three social media platforms: **X (Twitter)**, **Bluesky**, and **Mastodon**.

> **Figure:** Logos of X (Twitter), Bluesky, and Mastodon displayed side by side, illustrating the spectrum of platform choices from centralized to fully federated.

| Feature | X (Twitter) | Bluesky | Mastodon |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Private company, owned by Elon Musk | Independent Public Benefit LLC | Federation of independent, community-run servers |
| **Core Technology** | Centralized platform | Decentralized, uses AT Protocol | Decentralized, uses ActivityPub protocol |
| **User Data Control** | Company-controlled | User-portable, more user control | Users choose trusted servers, can migrate |
| **Content Moderation** | Centralized, corporate | Customizable moderation, user-defined services | Per-server (instance) moderation (community-led) |
| **Joining** | Open to all | Open signups (was invite-only) | Open, anyone can join or start a server |
| **Interface** | Twitter-style timeline | Twitter-like, familiar | Similar, but varies by app/server |
| **Federation/Interoperability** | None (siloed) | Partial: based on protocol, planned federation | Fully federated across the "Fediverse" |
| **Open Source** | Mostly closed-source | Open core/protocol, open API | Open source (core software and many apps) |
| **Account Migration** | Not supported | Will support migration via protocol | Supported; migrate account/data between servers |
| **Community/Developers** | Limited input | Developer/community-friendly | Highly community-driven, many independent developers |
